Reading and learning differences

Dyslexia

Long blocks of text, uneven spacing and fancy styling make words harder to track, especially for people with dyslexia. Here is a made-up campus notice.

Up to 1 in 10 people show signs of dyslexia. Estimates vary.

British Dyslexia Association

Try it

Try to find the date and the three things to bring, then switch on the fixes.

campuslife.example/notices

IMPORTANT INFORMATION REGARDING THE UPCOMING ORIENTATION PROGRAMME FOR ALL INCOMING FIRST YEAR STUDENTS

All incoming first year students are hereby informed that the orientation programme will commence on Monday the twelfth of October at eight o'clock in the morning at the main auditorium and attendance is compulsory for all students without exception, and students are required to bring along their admission letter, a valid national identification card such as the Ghana Card and two recent passport photographs which will be used to process student identification cards, and failure to present these documents may result in delays in the registration process which could affect access to accommodation and course registration, therefore all students are strongly encouraged to arrive early and to direct any enquiries to the Campus Life Office.

Orientation for first-year students

When: Monday 12 October, 8:00 am
Where: Main auditorium

Orientation is compulsory. Please bring:

  1. Your admission letter
  2. Your Ghana Card
  3. Two passport photos

Without these, your student ID, room and course registration may be delayed. Questions? Ask the Campus Life Office.

  1. 1

    One long wall of text. The key details are buried in a single 100-word sentence.

    Short chunks. A heading, short paragraphs and a numbered list.

  2. 2

    Justified and italic. Uneven gaps between words and slanted letters are hard to track.

    Left-aligned, regular text. Even spacing and upright letters.

  3. 3

    Shouty capitals and tight lines. Capitals remove word shapes, and lines crowd together.

    Sentence case and room to breathe. Wider line height and a little extra letter spacing.

  4. 4

    Important facts hidden. The date and the items to bring are hard to find.

    Key facts stand out. Dates are bold and the items are a list.

Who this affects

Dyslexia is a common learning difference that affects reading and spelling. It has nothing to do with intelligence. Clear layout also helps people with ADHD, people reading in a second language, and anyone skimming on a phone.

Using real headings and lists in your HTML also helps screen reader users jump straight to the part they need.

Tips for your own pages

  • Keep paragraphs short and use headings to break up text.
  • Use lists for steps and items.
  • Align text to the left. Avoid text-align: justify.
  • Use a line height around 1.5 or more.
  • Avoid long stretches of italics or ALL CAPS.

The code behind the fix

.notice {
  text-align: left;      /* not justify */
  font-style: normal;    /* not italic */
  line-height: 1.75;
  letter-spacing: 0.02em;
  max-width: 40rem;      /* shorter lines are easier to follow */
}
