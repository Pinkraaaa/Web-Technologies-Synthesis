Motor and mobility

Motor

People with tremors, limited hand movement or no mouse at all need targets they can hit and a page they can use with a keyboard. Here is a made-up airtime top-up form.

Everyone browses one-handed sometimes: a broken arm, a baby on your hip, a bumpy trotro.

Who this affects

Motor disabilities include cerebral palsy, Parkinson's, arthritis, paralysis and injuries. Some people use only a keyboard, a switch device, voice control or a mouth stick. Big, well-spaced targets also help anyone holding a phone in one hand on a bumpy ride.

Good forms use a <label> for every control and a <fieldset> with a <legend> for groups, like the network choice above.

Tips for your own pages

  • Make buttons and links at least 44 by 44 pixels.
  • Leave space between links so people don't hit the wrong one.
  • Never remove the focus outline without a better one.
  • Make sure everything works with only Tab, Enter and Space.
  • Use labels, not placeholders, to name inputs.
