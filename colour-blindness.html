    Text and symbols added. Every seat says Free or Taken, with ✓ or ✕.

  2. 2

    The instruction names a colour. "Choose a green seat" is useless if you can't see green.

    The instruction names a state. "Choose a seat marked Free" works for everyone.

  3. 3

    No shape or pattern difference. Seats differ by colour and nothing else.

    Patterns help too. Taken seats get stripes and free seats get a white border.

Who this affects

About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have some kind of colour blindness. The most common type makes red and green hard to tell apart, which is exactly the pair used for "stop" and "go", "error" and "success", "taken" and "free".

Colour still helps. The rule is simply that it should never be the only signal.

Tips for your own pages

  • Pair colour with text, icons or patterns.
  • Underline links instead of relying on link colour alone.
  • Label form errors in words, not just with a red border.
  • Avoid instructions like "click the green button".
  • Test with a colour blindness simulator before you publish.
