Deaf and hard of hearing

Hearing

When important information only comes through sound, people who are Deaf or hard of hearing simply never receive it. Here is a flood warning from a made-up Ghanaian news site.

Try it

Turn your sound off, press play, and try to find out which roads will flood. Then flip the switch.

    The alert is written out. The same warning, readable at a glance.

Who this affects

More than 1.5 billion people live with some degree of hearing loss, according to the World Health Organization. Some are Deaf and use a sign language, such as Ghanaian Sign Language, as their first language. Others are hard of hearing and rely on captions and lip reading.

Captions also help people who are watching in a noisy place, on mute in class, or still learning English.

Tips for your own pages

  • Add captions to every video, and check they are accurate.
  • Offer a written transcript for audio and video.
  • Never put important information only in sound.
  • Give more than one way to contact you.
  • Write in plain language. For many Deaf readers, English is a second language.

The code behind the fix

The <track> element links a caption file to a video. The caption file is a plain text file ending in .vtt, with a start time, an end time and the words for each line. Sounds go in square brackets so Deaf viewers know about the thunder too.

<video controls poster="media/weather-poster.jpg">
  <source src="media/weather.mp4" type="video/mp4">
  <track kind="captions" src="media/weather.vtt" srclang="en" label="English" default>
</video>
